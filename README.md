# Magnetic Well — 2026-11-10

Open source under the IOF Attribution License v1.0, released 2026-11-12. Nothing here is a device, a product, or a test result. Nothing has been built or bought. The package was sent to three named recipients 2026-11-10 (Justin Asendorf, Bishop Tyler Carey, Raymond Turley).

## What this folder holds

- **LICENSE.md** — the IOF Attribution License v1.0: free for anyone to use, copy, modify, and build upon, with attribution.
- **magnetic-well-nanoscale-assessment.md** — the nanoscale extension of Gregory's magnetic well architecture (assessed in chat 2026-11-10). Carries the macro assessment forward briefly, checks current nanomaterials against each layer of the stack (SPIONs, FePt, CoFeB/permalloy, 2D magnets, magnetic MXenes, graphene/diamagnetic platelets, ferrofluids, magnetic inks, self-assembling structures), works the architecture up as a spray-on magnetic paint (formulation, application, limits, and its relation to the Adaptive Coating concept), assesses every plausible use with benefits and honest limits (memory/logic, sensors, medical, energy harvesting/wireless power, EMI shielding, catalysis, water treatment, data storage, quantum/spintronics, ferrofluid devices, robotics), states the key physics limits (superparamagnetism, thermal stability, field scaling, aggregation, safety), and closes with the three most promising directions plus a first coupon test.
- **magnetic-well-concept.md** — the canonical concept document ("The Magnetic Well," by Gregory Scott Davis, 2026-11-10): his principle stated prominently ("Magnetic materials stand in the well"), the stack defined layer by layer, the physics, the verified sim numbers, the 11-configuration variation table and recommendation, the nanoscale/paint summary, all uses, honest limits, and the first test — plus two dated addenda (the throwing star checked magnetic; the assessed build requirements and verified prices).
- **code/magnetic_well_sim.py** — 2D magnetostatic finite-difference solver for the macro stack (runnable: `python3 code/magnetic_well_sim.py`, ~4 min, writes its own outputs). Baseline verified: the yoke roughly doubles the top-face field (0.573 T vs 0.295 T bare magnet); the well is real (0.133 T dip over the badge centre, 0.235 T lobes over the star tips, depth 0.102 T); star-tip concentration ratio 2.72 at 1 mm past the tip.
- **sim-results/** — SIMULATION.md (model, assumptions, comparison table, verification checks, limits, recommendation), results.json (all numbers for 11 configurations), field_map.png, centerline_profile.png, well_profile.png, baseline_fields.npz. Variations tested: yoke material (cast iron, low-carbon steel, permalloy — agree within 1%, saturation leaves extra permeability nothing to work on), cup yoke (strongest top field, +20%, but destroys the well), star points (sharp 5-point deepest well; 8-point/blunted read higher right at the tips), centre material (graphite 40× copper's push, still not a levitator), spacer (removing the aluminum cross changes nothing for a static field), magnet shape (square vs cylinder within 1% at badge height). Recommendation: flat cast-iron yoke + sharp 5-point star + pyrolytic graphite centre + standard cross + square magnet — essentially the build as described, with graphite swapped for the copper centre. Nothing in the stack levitates the badge; the rim's ferromagnetism holds it, the well centres it laterally.
- **magnetic-well-paint-coupon-test-plan.md** — the first physical test of the spray-on magnetic paint: 300×300 mm panels, formulations A (ferrite-only), B (full well paint: ferrite + star pattern + GNP skin), C (plain control), 2 panels each, plus optional D (ferrite + skin, no pattern); five measurements (M1 shielding in the TEM cell, M2 sheet resistance, M3 permeability, M4 adhesion, M5 Hall-probe field pattern) with proposed pass/fail criteria. Materials ≈ $670 for one coupon set. §8 assesses the macro build requirements from the simulation (yoke 55–70 mm square, 4–8 mm thick; clay 3–11 mm lateral, none in the vertical stack; star 55–65 mm span, 2.5–4 mm thick, sharp points; badge 55–65 mm diameter; magnet N52 to reproduce the simulated numbers exactly, N35 functional minimum) — assessed requirements, not measurements and not verified hardware values. The two previously unverified prices are verified in the plan: bulk Fe₃O₄ nanopowder 50–100 nm at Ossila, 250 g $285; GNP bulk $50–$500/kg.

## The macro well, in one line

Neodymium magnet → cast iron yoke → clay → aluminum cross → etched steel throwing star → grandfather's badge (copper star, steel rim) on top. The shaped field forms a magnetic well — a field minimum that seats the badge.
